Does Medicaid Cover Pain-Management Injections in Brooklyn?

By Pain Therapy Medical Care · Updated November 20, 2026 · 4 min read

Summary

Medicaid often covers medically necessary pain-management injections, including epidural steroid and cortisone injections, but exact coverage depends on your specific plan and the treatment being considered.

  • Medically necessary injections are commonly covered under NY Medicaid managed care plans.
  • Prior authorization is often required before certain injections are approved.
  • Having coverage verified before a visit avoids surprise costs afterwards.
  • Care of this kind is available across Brooklyn, the Five Towns, and Monsey.

If you have Medicaid and you are considering an injection for chronic pain, here is how coverage typically works and how to find out what applies to you before you book.

Does Medicaid cover pain-management injections?

In many cases, yes. New York Medicaid and Medicaid managed care plans generally cover pain-management injections, including epidural steroid injections and cortisone injections, when they are medically necessary and clinically appropriate for the diagnosis. Exactly which injections are covered, and under what circumstances, depends on the specific Medicaid plan, so this is usually confirmed before a treatment plan is recommended rather than assumed.

Do I need prior authorization for an injection?

Often, yes. Many injection treatments require prior authorization from the Medicaid plan before they are approved, which means the provider needs to submit documentation showing the treatment is medically necessary. This is a standard part of how Medicaid plans manage these treatments rather than a rule set by any one practice. The clinic usually handles the submission, and it is worth asking whether approval is still pending before treatment starts.

Do I need a referral to be seen for an injection consultation?

It depends on the specific Medicaid plan. Some plans require a referral from a primary care provider before seeing a specialist for injection-based pain treatment, while others do not. Confirming what a plan requires before the visit avoids a delay at check-in.

How do you verify my Medicaid coverage before treatment?

Before an injection is recommended or scheduled, the usual steps are checking the specific Medicaid plan, confirming what is covered and in-network, and identifying whether prior authorization or a referral is needed. Taken in that order, the coverage picture and any extra steps are clear before a treatment plan is agreed rather than after the fact.

What types of injections are typically covered?

Commonly covered injections, when medically necessary, include epidural steroid injections for sciatica or nerve-related back pain, and cortisone or corticosteroid injections for joint or tendon pain. Coverage for a specific injection depends on the diagnosis and the plan’s own rules, so the details are worth confirming for that exact situation rather than relying on general assumptions about what Medicaid typically covers.

What should I bring to help with the verification process?

Bring the Medicaid card, along with any referral a primary care provider has already issued. If the same condition has already been evaluated by another provider, records or imaging from that visit can help speed up both the coverage verification and the first appointment.

Coverage works differently under other plans, including Healthfirst, MetroPlusHealth, and Medicare.

Frequently asked questions

Which Medicaid plans do you accept?
Medicaid and a range of Medicaid managed care plans are widely accepted for pain management in Brooklyn. Coverage is worth confirming under the exact plan name before scheduling.

What if my injection needs prior authorization?
The clinic usually handles that process with the insurer. It is worth asking whether anything is still pending before treatment goes ahead.

Will I know if I owe anything out of pocket?
Most clinics will verify benefits before a visit if asked, which gives a clear picture of coverage and any costs in advance.

What if a specific injection isn’t covered under my plan?
Asking about this before a visit rather than after means an uncovered injection is a decision made with the cost known, and other treatment options can be discussed at the same time.

Can I get an injection at any of your Brooklyn locations?
Availability can differ between offices, so it is worth confirming both availability and coverage for a preferred location, whether Williamsburg, Flatbush, Five Towns, or Monsey.
