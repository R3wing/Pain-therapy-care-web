Fluid on the Knee (Water on the Knee): Why It Keeps Coming Back

By Pain Therapy Medical Care · Updated November 18, 2026 · 5 min read

TL;DR

Fluid on the knee, often called water on the knee, is extra fluid inside the knee joint. Draining it relieves pressure, but the fluid usually returns unless the cause is treated.

  • Osteoarthritis, injuries and gout are the most common causes.
  • The drained fluid can be tested, and that result is often the key to stopping the cycle.
  • Lasting relief comes from treating the cause: arthritis care, strengthening, weight management or gout treatment.
  • A hot, red, very painful knee with fever needs same-day care.

What is fluid on the knee?

Fluid on the knee is a buildup of extra fluid inside the knee joint, called a knee effusion. A healthy knee holds only a small amount of fluid to keep it moving smoothly. When the joint is irritated, injured or inflamed, it makes much more.

Common signs:

  • Swelling around or above the kneecap
  • A tight, heavy or puffy feeling in the joint
  • Stiffness, especially when bending the knee fully
  • Pain with walking, stairs or kneeling

What causes fluid on the knee?

The most common cause in adults over 50 is osteoarthritis, the gradual wear of the cartilage that cushions the joint. See knee osteoarthritis. Other causes include:

  • Injuries such as a meniscus tear or ligament sprain. Swelling within a few hours of an injury can mean bleeding inside the joint.
  • Gout or pseudogout, where crystals form in the joint and cause sudden, severe swelling.
  • Rheumatoid arthritis and other inflammatory conditions.
  • Infection, which is less common but serious.
  • Overuse, especially in people who kneel or squat for work.

Swelling only in front of the kneecap, outside the joint, is usually bursitis rather than fluid inside the knee.

Can you have a swollen knee without pain?

Yes. A knee can swell with little or no pain, especially with mild osteoarthritis or a slow buildup of fluid. It still deserves a check if it lasts more than a few days or keeps returning, because the cause may be getting worse quietly.

Why does fluid come back after the knee is drained?

Fluid comes back because draining treats the swelling, not the reason for it. Draining, called aspiration, uses a needle to remove fluid. It eases pressure and pain quickly. But if arthritis, a meniscus tear or gout is still active, the joint keeps producing extra fluid.

What can the drained fluid tell you?

The fluid itself is one of the most useful clues. A lab can check it for:

  • Crystals, which point to gout or pseudogout
  • Bacteria and white blood cells, which point to infection
  • Blood, which suggests an injury inside the joint
  • Signs of inflammation, which can suggest an inflammatory arthritis

If your knee has been drained more than once, ask whether the fluid was tested and what it showed. That answer often decides the treatment that finally stops the swelling.

What treatments help fluid on the knee?

The right treatment depends on the cause. Common options include:

  • Rest, ice, compression and elevation for flare-ups
  • Anti-inflammatory medicines, if they are safe for you
  • A steroid injection after draining, to calm inflammation. See steroid injections.
  • Gel (hyaluronic acid) injections for knee osteoarthritis. See HA knee injections.
  • Physical therapy to strengthen the thigh and hip muscles that support the knee. See physical therapy.
  • Weight management, since extra body weight adds load to the knee with every step
  • Gout treatment with medicines that lower uric acid
  • Surgery, only when an injury or advanced arthritis does not respond to other care

Many people manage knee swelling without surgery. See knee pain treatment without surgery.

When is fluid on the knee an emergency?

Get same-day care for:

  • A knee that is hot, red and very painful
  • Fever or chills with knee swelling
  • Swelling within hours of an injury, or a knee that gives way
  • Being unable to put weight on the leg

These can be signs of infection, a fracture or a serious injury.

What kind of doctor treats knee swelling?

A primary care doctor, a pain management doctor, a rheumatologist or an orthopedic doctor can evaluate a swollen knee. Pain management and rheumatology focus on non-surgical care; orthopedics also offers surgery. For repeated swelling, choose someone who can test the fluid and treat the cause. See knee pain.

Frequently asked questions

Can fluid on the knee go away on its own?

Mild swelling from a minor strain can settle with rest, ice and elevation. Swelling that lasts or returns usually needs the cause treated.

Does draining the knee hurt?

A numbing medicine is usually used first, so most people feel pressure rather than sharp pain.

How many times can a knee be drained?

There is no fixed number. Needing it drained repeatedly is a sign the cause needs more attention.

Should I walk with fluid on my knee?

Gentle walking is usually fine if it does not increase pain or swelling. Avoid kneeling, deep squats and impact until it settles.

Does a knee brace help with fluid on the knee?

A compression sleeve can help control swelling and give support during activity. It does not treat the cause.

Is heat or ice better for a swollen knee?

Ice is usually better for new or flaring swelling. Heat can ease stiffness once swelling has calmed down.
