Knee Pain Treatment in Brooklyn Without Surgery

By Pain Therapy Medical Care · Updated November 20, 2026 · 4 min read

Summary

Knee pain is one of the most common reasons people see a pain specialist, and most cases are managed without surgery. A physician-led evaluation finds the cause so the treatment matches it.

  • Common causes include arthritis, tendon and ligament strain, and wear inside the joint.
  • Non-surgical options include physical therapy, gel (hyaluronic acid) injections, and advanced modalities.
  • See a doctor if the knee is swollen, unstable, or the pain has lasted more than a few weeks.
  • Knee pain is treated at clinics across Brooklyn, the Five Towns, and Monsey, and many plans including Medicaid managed care are widely accepted.

Knee pain has a way of shrinking your world. Stairs, standing, and walking any distance become things you plan around. The good news is that most knee pain is managed without surgery.

What causes knee pain?

Knee pain can come from arthritis and cartilage wear, strained tendons or ligaments, or irritation inside the joint. Age, activity, and past injuries all play a part. Because the causes differ, the right treatment depends on which one is driving the pain. There is more detail on this knee pain page.

When should you see a doctor for knee pain?

Get evaluated if your knee is swollen, if it feels unstable or locks, if you cannot put weight on it, or if the pain has lasted more than a few weeks. Pain that follows an injury also deserves a proper look.

How is knee pain treated without surgery?

Most patients improve with a non-surgical, drug-free plan. Depending on the cause, that can include physical therapy to build strength and stability, gel (hyaluronic acid) injections to lubricate and cushion the joint, precise ultrasound-guided injections, and advanced modalities such as shockwave therapy.

What to expect at a first visit

At a physician-led practice, a doctor assesses the knee first, identifies the likely source, and starts with the least invasive approach that fits the case. Options should be explained plainly, and a good clinician will say when something outside their scope would serve a patient better.

Do you accept my insurance?

Many pain management clinics in the area work with Medicaid managed care, Healthfirst, Fidelis Care, MetroPlus, and Medicare. Coverage varies by specific plan, so it is worth confirming with the clinic before a visit.

Frequently asked questions

Do gel injections for the knee work?
For the right patient, hyaluronic acid injections can ease pain and improve movement by supporting the joint. An assessment tells you whether they are a good fit for your knee.

Will I need knee surgery?
Most people do not. Non-surgical care manages the majority of knee pain, and surgery is usually a last resort.

Does knee pain mean the cartilage is worn out?
Not necessarily. Cartilage changes show up on imaging in plenty of people without pain, and many painful knees show little structural change. Pain more often reflects how the joint is being loaded, the strength around it, and how sensitive the surrounding tissue has become.

Is it better to rest or keep moving with knee pain?
Controlled movement usually beats rest. Loading the joint within comfortable limits maintains cartilage health and muscle strength, both of which protect the knee. Activities that cause sharp pain, swelling afterward or a sense of giving way should be modified rather than continued unchanged.

Take the next step on your knee pain

You do not have to keep working around your knee. If knee pain has been holding on, a non-surgical assessment is the place to start.
