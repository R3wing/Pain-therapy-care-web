You may have heard people stating they have burning or shooting pain in the hands or feet, or you are experiencing such sensations in your joints. What does this mean? In terms of medical terminology, it is termed neuropathic pain. This is caused by nerve damage that changes the nerve function at the injured area and the region surrounding it.

The experience can be extremely painful. In the majority of the cases, the pain goes on its own, without requiring any serious medical intervention. With some pain therapy and OTC medications, you are good to go. However, if the pain persists or if it is diagnosed a lot later, this can create a lot of problems for you.

The primary reason is that neuropathic pain affects the joints and the surrounding area. So, it can hinder your movement, and thus, compromising your quality of life.

But with the introduction of cold laser therapy, people suffering from neuropathic pain have seen commendable results. If you are someone who has had enough of neuropathic pain, it is high time that you search for cold laser therapy near me because the results are promising.

Keep on reading to know more.

What is Neuropathic Pain?

When your sensory nervous system experiences any disease, trauma, or injury, the nerves within the system fails to work properly, compromising its duty to transmit sensation to the brain. When this happens, the affected area lacks sensation and leads to numbness. However, in other cases, it leads to a sense of pain in the injured region. This is called neuropathic pain, and if left untreated for longer durations, the pain can become unbearable.

It is important to understand that neuropathic pain doesn’t start abruptly, nor it resolves quickly. You will have to let yourself in for dedicated therapy. In extreme cases, you may not be able to move certain body parts, especially if the injury has occurred in the joints. Choosing the right treatment is important because how you lead the rest of your life will completely depend on that.

Most people avoid having surgeries because they can worsen the condition. Pain management with Brooklyn NY laser therapy has emerged as a viable treatment option for patients. Laser therapy for neuropathic pain has shown some incredible results.

What Causes Neuropathic Pain and How Cold Laser Therapy for Neck Pain can Help?

There are numerous reasons why you may develop neuropathic pain in the neck or any other body part. Nevertheless, on a cellular level, it can be because of an increased release of specific neurotransmitters that signals pains, coupled with a disability of nerves to regulate these neurotransmitters can lead to a sensation of pain in the affected region.

In addition to that, in the spinal cord, the cells that are responsible for interpreting painful signals are completely rearranged, which leads to the loss of normally-functioning cell bodies. This leads to a perception of pain, even if the external stimulus is absent. Over time, as the cells continue to perish, the sense of pain increases.

How can laser therapy help?

The best way to treat neuropathy pain is to promote nerve regrowth. While it may seem impossible, medical science has advanced to that phase where nothing seems impossible.

There is one treatment that can prove to be beneficial in the treatment of neuropathic pain. The treatment is called low-level laser therapy (LLLT), and it has been extremely useful in the management of musculoskeletal pain and pain associated with nerve damage.

Light is an essential part of all living things. Based on this simple fact, cold laser therapy was developed. Cold laser therapy uses low wavelengths of light to power the damaged cells and encourages them to regrow.

These lasers produce light energies that are invisible to the naked eye. Since human cells have the ability to absorb and emit light particles, which is called photons, cold laser therapy for neck pain works by locally producing a specific wavelength of light into the targeted cells, or in this case, the actual nerves.

When this happens, the powerhouse of the cells, also known as mitochondria, absorbs the light energy and is triggered to work faster. This increases the recovery in the cells of the nerves.

How long does the procedure take?

It depends on the severity of your condition, the wavelength of the laser used, and the area being treated. Nonetheless, it may easily take up to 3-4 visits to the doctor to complete the therapy. When looking for cold laser therapy near me, always choose a certified clinic.

Frequently asked questions

Why does nerve pain respond differently from muscle pain?
Because the mechanism differs. Muscle pain reflects tissue that is strained or overloaded and settles as that resolves. Nerve pain comes from damaged or irritated nerve fibers signaling incorrectly, so it can persist without any ongoing tissue damage and often does not respond to treatments aimed at muscle.

How might laser therapy affect an irritated nerve?
Published research describes effects on cellular energy production, local circulation and inflammatory signaling in treated tissue, all of which influence how a nerve behaves. It is a supporting intervention rather than a repair mechanism, and the evidence is stronger for some nerve conditions than others.

Is laser therapy used on its own for nerve pain?
Rarely. Nerve pain usually has a driver, whether compression, a metabolic cause such as diabetes, or a vitamin deficiency, and treating the symptom without addressing that limits results. Laser is generally one component alongside work on the underlying cause.

Which kinds of nerve pain are least likely to respond?
Nerve pain from significant structural compression tends to need the compression addressed first. Long-standing damage with substantial fiber loss also responds less readily than recent irritation. Both are identified at assessment, which is why realistic expectations are set before a course starts.
