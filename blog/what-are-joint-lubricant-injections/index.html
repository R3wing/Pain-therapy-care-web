Joint Lubricant (Gel) Injections for Knee Pain: How They Work, Cost and What to Expect

By Pain Therapy Medical Care · Updated November 20, 2026 · 8 min read

TL;DR

Joint lubricant injections, also called gel, hyaluronic acid (HA) or viscosupplement injections, add a thick lubricating fluid to a knee with osteoarthritis.

  • They can ease pain and stiffness for some people, usually starting within a few weeks and lasting up to about six months.
  • They do not rebuild cartilage or cure arthritis, and results vary. Medical guidelines are mixed on how much they help.
  • Some products are a single shot; others are a series of 3 to 5 weekly shots.
  • Medicare and many New York Medicaid plans cover them for knee osteoarthritis when criteria are met.
  • They work best alongside exercise, physical therapy and weight management.

What are joint lubricant injections?

Joint lubricant injections put hyaluronic acid, a gel-like substance, into the knee joint. Hyaluronic acid occurs naturally in healthy joint fluid, where it helps the joint glide and absorb shock. In knee osteoarthritis, the joint fluid becomes thinner and less cushioning. The injection supplements that fluid, which is why it is also called viscosupplementation.

You may hear them called:

  • Gel injections or knee gel shots
  • Hyaluronic acid (HA) injections
  • Viscosupplement injections
  • Brand names such as Synvisc-One, Euflexxa, Durolane, Monovisc, Gel-One, Orthovisc or Hyalgan

What lubricates the knee joint naturally?

Synovial fluid lubricates the knee. It is made by the lining of the joint and contains hyaluronic acid, which gives it a slippery, gel-like texture. Movement circulates this fluid and helps nourish cartilage, which has no blood supply of its own. That is one reason regular, gentle movement matters so much for arthritic knees.

How can you keep your knee joints lubricated without an injection?

You cannot add fluid to the knee by eating or drinking something, but you can help the joint work better:

  • Move often. Walking, cycling and water exercise keep joint fluid circulating. See aqua therapy.
  • Strengthen the thigh and hip muscles to take load off the joint. See physical therapy.
  • Lose extra weight if you carry it; every pound adds several pounds of force to the knee with each step.
  • Warm up stiff knees with heat or a few minutes of easy movement before activity.
  • Be cautious with supplements. Major arthritis guidelines do not recommend glucosamine or chondroitin for knee osteoarthritis because the evidence is weak.

Who are joint lubricant injections for?

They are mainly used for knee osteoarthritis that still hurts after basic treatments. A doctor may suggest them if you:

  • Have knee osteoarthritis confirmed by exam and X-ray
  • Still have pain despite exercise, physical therapy, weight management or pain relievers
  • Cannot take anti-inflammatory medicines, or they do not help enough
  • Want to delay knee replacement or are not ready for surgery

They are not designed for knee pain from a ligament tear, a locked knee, or pain coming from the hip or lower back. See what doctor to see for knee pain.

Do knee gel injections work?

They help some people and not others. Studies show modest average benefit, and some people get several months of meaningful relief while others notice little change.

What the guidelines say (honest version):

  • The American College of Rheumatology and Arthritis Foundation (2019) conditionally recommend against routine use for knee osteoarthritis because the average benefit in studies is small.
  • The American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons (2021) does not recommend routine use.
  • In practice, many doctors still offer them to selected patients who have not done well with other treatments, and Medicare covers them under set criteria.

The bottom line: they are an option to discuss, not a guaranteed fix, and they work best as part of a full plan. See knee osteoarthritis: non-surgical options.

How many gel injections do you need: one shot or a series?

It depends on the product. Some are given as a single injection; others as a series:

Type Typical schedule Examples
Single injection 1 shot Synvisc-One, Durolane, Monovisc, Gel-One
Short series 3 shots, one week apart Euflexxa, Orthovisc (3 to 4)
Longer series 5 shots, one week apart Hyalgan, Supartz

Your doctor and your insurance plan usually decide which product is used. Neither type has been shown to be clearly better than the other.

How long do joint lubricant injections last?

Relief, when it happens, usually starts within 2 to 5 weeks and can last up to about six months. A course can be repeated if it helped; Medicare generally allows a repeat course no sooner than six months after the last one.

What happens during a gel injection?

The injection is done in the office and takes a few minutes:

  1. The skin is cleaned and may be numbed.
  2. If the knee is swollen, extra fluid may be drawn out first.
  3. The gel is injected into the joint, sometimes with ultrasound guidance for accuracy. See ultrasound-guided injections.
  4. You can usually walk out and return to normal light activity the same day.

Most doctors advise avoiding heavy exercise, long standing and heavy lifting for about 48 hours.

What are the side effects of knee gel injections?

Most side effects are mild and short-lived:

  • Pain, warmth or swelling in the knee for a day or two
  • Mild stiffness or a feeling of fullness

Less common: a flare of swelling and pain lasting a few days. Rare but serious: infection. Call your doctor promptly if the knee becomes hot, red and very painful, or if you develop a fever.

Who should not get gel injections?

You may not be a candidate if you have:

  • An infection in or near the knee
  • A skin condition at the injection site
  • An allergy to hyaluronic acid products; some products are made from rooster combs, so tell your doctor about any allergy to birds, feathers or egg products
  • Knee pain from a cause other than osteoarthritis

Tell your doctor if you take blood thinners.

How much do knee gel injections cost, and does insurance cover them?

Cost depends mostly on your insurance. Medicare Part B covers hyaluronic acid injections for knee osteoarthritis when medical criteria are met, such as confirmed osteoarthritis and other treatments tried first. Many New York Medicaid managed care plans, including Healthfirst, Fidelis Care and MetroPlusHealth, also cover them, sometimes with prior authorization. Without insurance, the product alone can cost several hundred dollars or more per course, so check coverage before your visit. See does Medicare cover pain management and does Medicaid cover pain-management injections.

Gel injection or cortisone shot: what is the difference?

They do different jobs. A cortisone (steroid) injection reduces inflammation and often works within days, but relief usually lasts weeks to a few months. A gel injection supplements joint fluid; relief, when it comes, builds over weeks and may last longer. Doctors sometimes use cortisone for a flare and consider gel when pain keeps returning. See steroid injections and how often you can get a cortisone shot.

Can gel injections help you avoid knee replacement?

There is no proof they prevent knee replacement, but for some people they ease pain enough to stay active and delay surgery. The strongest tools for delaying replacement are exercise and weight management. See how to avoid or delay knee replacement.

Frequently asked questions

Is a gel injection the same as lubricant injected into the knee?

Yes. “Joint lubricant injection” and “gel injection” usually mean the same thing: hyaluronic acid injected into the knee.

Do gel injections hurt?

Most people feel brief pressure or a sting. The skin is often numbed first, and the injection takes a few minutes.

Can I walk after a knee gel injection?

Yes. Most people walk out normally. Avoid strenuous activity for about 48 hours.

Can you get gel injections in other joints?

They are approved mainly for the knee. Use in other joints, such as the hip or shoulder, is less established; ask your doctor.

How soon will I feel a difference?

Usually within 2 to 5 weeks, if the injection helps.

What if the first gel injection did not help?

If one course did not help, another usually is not repeated. Your doctor may suggest other options.

Are gel injections safe if I have diabetes?

Generally yes, and unlike cortisone they do not usually raise blood sugar. Tell your doctor about your diabetes before treatment.

Where can I get hyaluronic acid injections?

They are offered by pain management, orthopedic, physical medicine and some rheumatology practices. Check that the provider is in network for your plan.
