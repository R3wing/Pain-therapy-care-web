How to Avoid or Delay Knee Replacement: What Actually Helps

By Pain Therapy Medical Care · Updated November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Summary

Many people with knee osteoarthritis delay knee replacement for years, and some never need it. The steps with the strongest evidence are simple, but they take consistency.

  • Exercise and physical therapy to strengthen the thigh and hip muscles are the most proven non-surgical treatments.
  • Weight loss reduces the load on the knee with every step.
  • Pain relief (topical or oral anti-inflammatories, steroid injections) helps you stay active.
  • Gel injections and newer therapies help some people; results vary.
  • Replacement is worth considering when pain limits daily life despite months of good non-surgical care.

Can you avoid knee replacement?

Often you can delay it, and sometimes avoid it. Knee replacement is usually recommended for advanced osteoarthritis that keeps limiting walking, sleep and daily activities despite non-surgical treatment. Arthritis does not progress at the same speed in everyone, and many people manage their symptoms for years. Nobody can promise a knee will never need surgery, but the steps below give you the best chance. See knee osteoarthritis: symptoms and stages.

Which exercises help you avoid knee replacement?

Strengthening exercises are the most proven non-surgical treatment for knee arthritis. Strong quadriceps (front of the thigh) and hip muscles take pressure off the joint. Helpful options include:

  • Straight-leg raises and seated leg extensions
  • Sit-to-stand from a chair
  • Side-lying hip raises and glute bridges
  • Low-impact cardio: walking, stationary cycling, water exercise

A physical therapist can build a plan that matches your pain level and progress it safely. See physical therapy and aqua therapy.

How much does weight loss help the knee?

A lot. Each pound of body weight puts several pounds of force through the knee with every step. For people with extra weight, losing even 5 to 10 percent of body weight can noticeably reduce knee pain and improve function. Weight loss works best combined with exercise.

What pain treatments help you stay active?

Pain control matters because it lets you keep moving. Options include:

  • Topical anti-inflammatory gels on the knee
  • Oral anti-inflammatories, if your doctor says they are safe for you
  • Steroid (cortisone) injections for flare-ups; relief usually lasts weeks to a few months. See steroid injections and how often you can get a cortisone shot.
  • A cane in the hand opposite the painful knee, or a knee brace for support

Do gel (hyaluronic acid) injections prevent knee replacement?

There is no proof that gel injections prevent knee replacement, but some people get months of pain relief from them. Medical guidelines are mixed: some do not recommend them routinely, while others see a place for them in selected patients. If you are considering them, ask about your expected benefit and cost. See HA knee injections and gel knee injections: cost and what to expect.

What about shockwave, laser and other newer therapies?

Technology-based therapies such as shockwave, laser and TECAR are used to ease knee pain and support rehab. Research is still developing, and they work best as part of a plan that includes exercise. See shockwave therapy and knee pain treatment without surgery.

What daily habits protect your knees?

  • Keep moving through the day; long sitting stiffens arthritic knees
  • Choose supportive, cushioned shoes
  • Use rails on stairs and avoid deep squats and kneeling during flares
  • Use heat for stiffness and ice for swelling
  • Manage blood sugar if you have diabetes, since it affects joints and healing

When is knee replacement the right choice?

Knee replacement is worth discussing when severe pain and stiffness keep limiting walking, sleep or daily tasks despite several months of consistent non-surgical care, and X-rays show advanced arthritis. It is a highly successful surgery for the right patient. Choosing it is not a failure; it is a decision about quality of life. If you still have pain after a replacement, see why do I still have pain after knee replacement? (publishes Jan 4).

Frequently asked questions

Can you reverse knee arthritis?

Cartilage loss cannot be reversed, but pain and function often improve a lot with exercise, weight loss and treatment.

At what age do most people get a knee replacement?

Most knee replacements are done in people in their 60s and 70s, but age matters less than symptoms and X-ray findings.

Is walking good for knees with arthritis?

Yes, for most people. Regular, comfortable walking keeps the joint moving and the muscles strong. Shorten the distance during flares.

Can a knee brace help me avoid surgery?

A brace can reduce pain and improve stability for some people, which helps them stay active. It does not stop arthritis.

How long can you delay a knee replacement?

It varies. Some people delay it for years with good non-surgical care; others reach the point where surgery makes sense sooner.

Does insurance cover non-surgical knee treatment?

Most plans cover physical therapy and injections, sometimes with prior authorization. Check with your plan. See [does Medicare cover pain management](https://paintherapycare.com/blog/medicare-pain-management-brooklyn/).
