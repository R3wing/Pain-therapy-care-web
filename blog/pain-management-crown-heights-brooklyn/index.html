If you live in Crown Heights and you are dealing with ongoing back, knee, neck, or nerve pain, you do not have to travel into Manhattan or wait months for a specialist. Chronic pain care is available close to home, and for most patients, surgery is not the first step.

Where can Crown Heights residents get pain management care?

Crown Heights residents have several Brooklyn options for evaluation and treatment. Most insurance plans do not require a referral to start the process, and appointments at an outpatient clinic are often available sooner than at a hospital-based specialty clinic.

Many patients arrive after trying medication or physical therapy alone without enough relief. Others come earlier, before considering injections or surgery. Either way, care starts with an evaluation to understand what is actually driving the pain, not a generic treatment plan.

What conditions does pain management treat?

A pain management evaluation typically covers a wide range of chronic pain conditions, including:

  • Lower back pain
  • Sciatica, including pain patterns like lumbar spinal stenosis
  • Knee and leg pain
  • Neck pain
  • Nerve pain and neuropathy

Treatment is built around the condition actually driving the symptoms. A pain management visit usually starts with a physical evaluation and a review of history, with imaging ordered or reviewed when appropriate before a plan is recommended.

What drug-free treatment options are available?

Depending on your evaluation, your plan may include shockwave therapy, EMTT (Extracorporeal Magnetotransduction Therapy), Class IV laser therapy, or guided physical therapy. These are non-surgical, drug-free technologies that may be considered as part of a broader treatment plan for appropriate candidates. They are not a guaranteed fix for every condition, and your provider will explain what is realistic for your specific diagnosis before you start.

This combination of technologies is not something every Brooklyn clinic offers. If you have already tried standard physical therapy or medication without enough relief, these options may be worth discussing at your evaluation.

What if I’ve already tried physical therapy or medication without relief?

You are not out of options. Many people seek pain management specifically because standard physical therapy or medication alone did not resolve their pain. When that happens, it usually means the plan needs to change, not that nothing can help. A thorough evaluation focuses on identifying what was missed the first time, whether that is an underlying diagnosis that was not fully addressed, a technology option that was not part of the original plan, or a need for a different combination of treatments altogether.

What insurance plans do you accept?

Medicaid, Medicaid managed care plans, Healthfirst, Fidelis Care, MetroPlus, and Medicare are commonly accepted for pain management in Brooklyn. Coverage varies by specific plan, so it is worth having benefits verified before a visit rather than after. For Healthfirst specifically, here is what to expect with referrals and prior authorization in more detail.

What does a first visit look like?

Your first visit centers on a full evaluation, not a quick prescription. Expect a review of your pain history, a physical exam, and a discussion of what has and has not helped so far. If you have prior imaging or records, bring them, since they can save time and help your provider build a more complete picture. By the end of the visit, you should have a clear sense of what is likely driving your pain and what the first steps of your plan look like.

Do I need a referral to be seen?

It depends on the specific insurance plan. Some plans require a referral from a primary care provider before seeing a specialist, and others do not. Checking with the plan or the clinic before scheduling avoids a wasted visit.

How is this different from just seeing your primary care doctor?

A primary care doctor is a good starting point, but pain management is a focused specialty. Pain specialists spend more time on the specific mechanics of the pain, work with non-surgical technologies like shockwave, EMTT, and laser therapy, and build treatment plans specifically around chronic pain rather than general health maintenance. Many patients see a pain specialist alongside their primary care doctor, not instead of them.

Frequently asked questions

How close is your clinic to Crown Heights?
Pain management clinics operate across Brooklyn, the Five Towns, and Monsey. From Crown Heights, a Brooklyn location is usually the easiest to reach, so it is worth comparing travel times before booking.

Is chronic pain ever fully resolved, or only managed?
Both outcomes occur. Where a clear mechanical driver is found and addressed, pain can resolve rather than simply be contained. Where the nervous system has become persistently sensitized, the realistic goal is often substantial reduction and restored function rather than complete absence of pain.

How soon can I be seen?
Appointment availability varies, but outpatient pain clinics generally see new patients sooner than hospital-based specialty clinics. Calling ahead gives the clearest picture of the next available time.

How is progress measured during chronic pain treatment?
Usually against function as well as pain level. Distance walked, hours slept, activities resumed and medication reduced are all more informative than a single rating out of ten. Setting those markers early is what makes it possible to tell whether a plan is working or needs changing.

Does Medicaid cover non-surgical pain treatments in New York?
Many are covered, though the detail depends on the managed care plan and on whether the treatment is judged medically necessary. Some technology-assisted modalities sit outside standard coverage. Checking the specific treatment against the specific plan before starting is what avoids an unexpected bill.
