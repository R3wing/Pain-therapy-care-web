Does Healthfirst Cover Pain Management in Brooklyn? Network, Referral & Prior-Authorization Guide

By Pain Therapy Medical Care · Updated November 20, 2026 · 4 min read

Summary

Healthfirst often covers medically necessary pain management, but the exact benefits, referral rules, and prior-authorization requirements depend on your specific plan.

  • Coverage depends on your exact Healthfirst plan and what is medically necessary.
  • Some services may need a referral or prior authorization, depending on your plan type.

If you have Healthfirst insurance and you are dealing with chronic pain, you likely want to know what is actually covered before you book an appointment. Here is how to find out.

Does Healthfirst cover pain management?

In many cases, yes. Healthfirst plans generally cover pain management services that are medically necessary, which can include evaluation, physical therapy, and certain injections. Exactly what is covered, and under what conditions, depends on your specific plan, since Healthfirst offers several plan types with different benefit structures.

Do I need a referral with Healthfirst?

It depends on the plan type. Some Healthfirst plans require a referral from a primary care provider before seeing a specialist, while others do not. Checking what a specific plan requires before the visit, rather than at check-in, avoids a delay or a rescheduled appointment.

What about prior authorization?

Certain treatments, particularly some injections or advanced imaging, may require prior authorization from Healthfirst before they are approved. This is a standard step in insurance billing rather than a rule set by any one practice. The clinic usually submits the request, and asking whether approval has come through before the appointment avoids arriving without an answer. The approved options are listed in Medicaid-approved therapies for pain management.

How do you verify my Healthfirst coverage?

Before a visit, the usual steps are checking the specific Healthfirst plan, confirming what is in-network, and identifying any referral or authorization steps needed. Taken in advance, that means fewer surprises and a clearer picture of likely costs ahead of the appointment.

Frequently asked questions

Does Healthfirst require a referral before seeing a pain specialist?
It depends on the Healthfirst plan. Several Healthfirst products are managed care plans that route specialist visits through a primary care provider, while others do not. Because the requirement is set at plan level rather than across the insurer, the plan name on the member card is what settles it.

How can a Healthfirst member confirm a clinic is in network?
Network status can be checked through the Healthfirst member portal or by calling the number on the card, and most clinics will verify it on request. Confirming under the exact plan name matters, because a provider can be in network for one Healthfirst product and out of network for another.

What does Healthfirst prior authorization involve for pain treatment?
It is a coverage review carried out before certain treatments proceed. The clinic submits clinical documentation supporting medical necessity, and the plan approves, denies or requests more information. Treatment scheduled before that decision returns can be delayed, so confirming the status in advance is worthwhile.

Which Healthfirst plans do you accept?
Multiple Healthfirst plan types are commonly accepted for pain management in Brooklyn. Coverage is worth confirming under the exact plan name before scheduling.

What happens if Healthfirst denies coverage for a pain treatment?
A denial can often be appealed, and plans are required to provide a written reason and an appeals process. In practice the options are to appeal with further documentation, switch to a covered treatment that addresses the same problem, or proceed privately with the cost understood upfront.

Take the next step

Insurance questions are not a reason to delay getting care. Healthfirst benefits can be checked against the specific plan before anything is scheduled.
