Chronic Prostatitis That Antibiotics Did Not Fix: The Pelvic Floor Connection

By Pain Therapy Medical Care · Updated November 8, 2026 · 4 min read

If you have been treated for prostatitis with round after round of antibiotics and the pain keeps coming back, it is worth asking whether infection is still the problem. Some men do have chronic bacterial prostatitis, a recurring infection that needs a urologist’s care. For many others, once infection has been ruled out, the pain is muscular and nerve-driven: a tight, overactive pelvic floor, not a bacterial one.

Why do antibiotics not fix chronic prostatitis?

Most chronic pelvic pain in men is chronic pelvic pain syndrome (CPPS), also called chronic non-bacterial prostatitis. With no active infection to clear, antibiotics do not resolve it. The pain usually starts with trigger points in the pelvic floor muscles. These tight knots can drive ongoing muscle spasm, and over time the nerves in the area become sensitized, so pain signals keep firing.

How do you know if your pelvic floor is the cause?

Common patterns include pelvic, perineal, or groin pain, pain or pressure that worsens with prolonged sitting, urinary urgency or frequency, and discomfort that flares with stress. Repeated negative infection workups are a strong clue, and your urologist’s opinion is the one to confirm it.

What actually helps male chronic pelvic pain?

Once urology has ruled out infection, the goal is to reduce pain signaling and let the pelvic floor muscles recover, restoring blood flow and oxygen to the tissue so the muscles can calm down. Male pelvic pain can be treated non-invasively and drug-free in Brooklyn, combining hands-on pelvic floor rehabilitation with shockwave therapy to ease spasm and quiet the nerves driving the pain. It is a different path, built for the men the usual approach leaves behind.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between prostatitis and CPPS?

Bacterial prostatitis is an infection, usually treated with antibiotics, though some chronic cases keep recurring or do not respond for various reasons. CPPS (chronic non-bacterial prostatitis) has no active infection, and the pain is driven by a tight, overactive pelvic floor and sensitized nerves.

Why does my prostatitis keep coming back after antibiotics?

If the cause is muscular and nerve-driven rather than bacterial, antibiotics cannot fix it, so the pain returns. That pattern points toward CPPS, though a urologist should first rule out chronic bacterial prostatitis.

Can pelvic floor therapy help chronic prostatitis?

For non-bacterial, muscle-driven pelvic pain, yes. Easing pelvic floor spasm and calming the nerves addresses the actual driver.

Does shockwave therapy help male pelvic pain?

Published research supports low-intensity shockwave for chronic pelvic pain syndrome and chronic non-bacterial prostatitis. It is non-invasive and drug-free.

Is chronic pelvic pain in men treatable without surgery or drugs?

Often, yes, once infection has been ruled out. Pelvic floor care with shockwave is a non-invasive, drug-free approach aimed at the muscular and nerve source of the pain.
