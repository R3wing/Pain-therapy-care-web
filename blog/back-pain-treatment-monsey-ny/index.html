If persistent low back pain is affecting your daily life and you live in the Monsey area, you have access to evaluation and non-surgical treatment without a long trip into the city for specialist care.

What are the common causes of low back pain?

Low back pain has several common causes, including muscle strain or spasm, degenerative disc changes, and, less commonly, more specific conditions like a herniated disc or lumbar spinal stenosis. Sciatica, pain radiating from the back into the leg, often points toward a nerve-related cause rather than a purely muscular one. The right treatment depends heavily on which of these is actually driving your pain, which is why an evaluation matters more than guessing based on symptoms alone.

What symptoms need prompt evaluation?

New or worsening leg weakness, numbness in the groin or inner thigh area, or any change in bowel or bladder function are reasons to be seen promptly rather than waiting. Gradual, activity-related back pain without these specific features can typically be addressed at a scheduled evaluation rather than urgent care.

What does treatment for back pain typically involve?

Most back pain responds well to non-surgical care. Depending on your evaluation, your plan may include targeted physical therapy, activity modification, and, for appropriate candidates, non-surgical technologies like shockwave or laser therapy as part of a broader plan. If your pain is radiating and nerve-related, treatments like epidural steroid injections may also be considered if conservative care alone isn’t providing enough relief. You can read more about two related patterns: muscle spasm versus a herniated disc and degenerative disc disease.

What insurance plans do you accept?

Medicaid, Medicaid managed care plans, Healthfirst, Fidelis Care, MetroPlus, and Medicare are commonly accepted for pain management in Brooklyn. Coverage varies by specific plan, so it is worth having benefits verified before a visit rather than after.

How do you decide between physical therapy, injections, or a non-surgical technology?

The right starting point depends on your specific diagnosis and how severe your symptoms are. Muscle-related and mild disc-related back pain often starts with targeted physical therapy, since building strength and correcting specific movement patterns is one of the best-supported treatments available. Patients whose pain hasn’t responded well enough to exercise alone, or who have more clearly nerve-related symptoms, may be candidates for an injection or a non-surgical technology as an additional option. Your provider builds this plan around your specific exam findings, not a default sequence applied to everyone the same way. The two approaches are weighed directly against each other in TECAR compared with injection for back pain.

What does a first visit for back pain involve?

Your first visit centers on a full evaluation: a review of your symptom history, a physical exam of your back’s strength, flexibility, and specific movement patterns, and a discussion of what has and has not helped so far. If you have prior imaging or records, bring them, since they can help build a more complete picture. By the end of the visit, you should have a clear sense of what is likely driving your pain and what your treatment options look like.

Frequently asked questions

Does persistent low back pain need a different approach from a sudden flare?
Generally yes. A sudden flare is usually managed by settling symptoms and restoring movement. Pain persisting beyond about three months is treated as a longer-term problem, where nerve sensitivity, deconditioning and daily load patterns matter more than the original injury, so the plan looks wider than the painful area.

Can low back pain come from the hip rather than the spine?
Yes. Hip joint problems often refer pain into the buttock, groin and outer thigh, and are regularly mistaken for a spinal source. Examination separates the two by testing hip rotation and spinal movement independently, which matters because treatment for a hip problem differs from treatment for a disc or nerve issue.

Does sitting for long periods make low back pain worse?
Often. Sustained sitting loads the lumbar discs and lets supporting muscle switch off, so pain frequently builds through a long drive or workday. Changing position regularly tends to help more than finding one perfect posture, and a pattern tied to sitting is useful detail at an evaluation.

What does morning stiffness in the lower back usually indicate?
Brief stiffness that eases within minutes of moving is common with mechanical back pain. Stiffness lasting more than half an hour most mornings, particularly in younger adults, is treated as a reason for further assessment, because it can point toward an inflammatory rather than mechanical cause.

Is bed rest useful for a low back flare?
Extended bed rest is generally discouraged. Beyond a day or two it tends to prolong recovery by stiffening joints and weakening muscle. Current guidance favors staying as active as symptoms reasonably allow, scaling activity down rather than stopping it, then building back up as the pain settles.
