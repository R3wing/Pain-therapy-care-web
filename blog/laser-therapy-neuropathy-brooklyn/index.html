Does Laser Therapy Help Neuropathy? What the Research Actually Shows

By Pain Therapy Medical Care · Updated September 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Summary

Research on laser therapy, technically photobiomodulation, for neuropathy shows promising but still developing evidence, with the strongest data in diabetic peripheral neuropathy. It is best considered an adjunct, not a standalone cure. Its mechanism in nerve pain is covered in laser therapy and neuropathic pain.

  • Systematic reviews show improvement in neuropathic pain and nerve conduction in some studies.
  • Evidence quality varies, and study designs are not yet fully standardized.
  • Laser therapy works best as part of a broader treatment plan, not a replacement for it.
  • Your provider can tell you honestly whether you’re a reasonable candidate.

If you are dealing with neuropathy and have heard laser therapy might help, here is an honest look at what the research actually shows, not just marketing claims.

What is laser therapy for neuropathy, exactly?

Laser therapy for neuropathy, more precisely called photobiomodulation therapy or low-level laser therapy, uses specific wavelengths of light applied to the skin over affected nerves. The proposed mechanism involves improving cellular energy production and reducing local inflammation in a way that may support nerve function and reduce pain signaling. It is a non-invasive, drug-free treatment, delivered in-office over a series of sessions rather than a single procedure.

What does the actual research say?

The evidence is genuinely promising but still developing, and it varies by condition. A systematic review focused specifically on diabetic peripheral neuropathy found that photobiomodulation therapy showed improvement in neuropathic pain and nerve conduction velocity in several included studies, along with reduced plantar pressure, a relevant factor in preventing foot complications. At the same time, researchers examining this body of literature have noted that differences in study design, laser parameters, and treatment protocols between studies make it difficult to draw a single definitive conclusion about exactly how much benefit to expect.

Does laser therapy work for every type of neuropathy?

Most of the stronger available research focuses specifically on diabetic peripheral neuropathy, not neuropathy in general. Evidence for other causes of neuropathy, including chemotherapy-induced neuropathy, is more preliminary, with smaller studies suggesting possible benefit but not yet enough robust evidence to call it an established, first-line treatment for those specific causes. This distinction matters, since a treatment that shows meaningful evidence for one type of neuropathy should not be assumed to work identically for a different underlying cause.

Is laser therapy a replacement for other neuropathy treatments?

No, and any provider being straightforward with you should say so clearly. Laser therapy is best understood as a possible adjunct within a broader treatment plan, not a standalone cure or a replacement for addressing the underlying cause of your neuropathy, such as blood sugar management for diabetic neuropathy or coordination with your oncology team for chemotherapy-induced neuropathy. Medications with stronger evidence for neuropathic pain, along with addressing the root cause where possible, remain the foundation of treatment, with laser therapy considered alongside them for appropriate candidates.

Are there risks or downsides to trying laser therapy?

Laser therapy is generally well tolerated with a low risk profile, since it is non-invasive and does not involve medication side effects or injection-related risks. The main downside is more about expectations than safety: because the evidence base is still developing, results vary between patients, and it should not be pursued as a reason to delay or skip more established treatments for your underlying condition. Your provider can give you a realistic sense of what to expect based on your specific diagnosis before you start. How do I know if I’m a reasonable candidate?

An evaluation looking at your specific type of neuropathy, its underlying cause, your prior treatment history, and your overall health picture is the right starting point before considering laser therapy. Your provider can tell you honestly whether the current evidence supports trying it for your specific situation, and how it would fit alongside whatever else is already part of your treatment plan, rather than presenting it as a guaranteed solution.

What should I ask before starting laser therapy for neuropathy?

Reasonable questions to bring to your evaluation include: what does the evidence actually show for your specific type of neuropathy, how many sessions would a reasonable trial involve before reassessing, and what specific improvement would count as a meaningful response. A provider being straightforward with you should be able to answer these directly rather than making broad promises, and should be willing to say if the evidence for your specific situation is more limited than for diabetic peripheral neuropathy specifically.

How does laser therapy fit alongside other technologies you offer?

For some patients, laser therapy is considered alongside other non-surgical technologies, like shockwave therapy or EMTT, depending on the specific condition being treated and where nerve pain overlaps with other issues like tendon or joint pain. These technologies work through different mechanisms and are not interchangeable, so your provider will explain which, if any, are appropriate for your specific diagnosis rather than defaulting to a single default treatment for every type of pain.

Frequently asked questions

How many sessions of laser therapy are typically needed?
This varies by individual and condition. Your provider will outline a specific plan and reassess your response along the way rather than committing to an indefinite, open-ended course.

Is laser therapy covered by insurance?
Coverage varies by plan and by how the treatment is classified. It is worth having specific coverage verified before any treatment is recommended or started rather than after.

Can laser therapy be combined with medication for neuropathy?
Yes, it is generally considered alongside, not instead of, other appropriate treatments, including medication when indicated.

Will laser therapy cure my neuropathy?
No treatment, including laser therapy, should be presented as a guaranteed cure. It is one possible tool within a broader, realistic treatment plan.

How is laser therapy thought to affect nerve pain?
Published research describes effects on cellular energy production, local circulation and inflammatory signaling within treated tissue, which are the proposed routes to reduced nerve pain. Evidence quality varies by condition and protocol, so likely results are discussed against a specific diagnosis rather than promised in general.
