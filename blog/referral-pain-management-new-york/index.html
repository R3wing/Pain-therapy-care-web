Do You Need a Referral to See a Pain Management Doctor in New York?

By Pain Therapy Medical Care · Updated November 19, 2026 · 5 min read

Summary

Whether you need a referral to see a pain management doctor in New York depends on your health plan, not on the doctor.

  • Original Medicare does not require referrals to see specialists.
  • Most HMO plans, including many Medicare Advantage HMOs, require a referral from your primary care doctor.
  • Medicaid managed care plans set their own rules, and some require a referral for certain specialists.
  • PPO plans usually do not require referrals.
  • The fastest way to know: call the member services number on your insurance card before you book.

Do you need a referral to see a pain management doctor?

It depends on your insurance plan. A referral is a written approval from your primary care provider (PCP) that sends you to a specialist. Some plans require one before they will pay for a specialist visit. Others let you book directly. If your plan needs a referral and you go without one, the visit may not be covered.

Which plans usually need a referral?

Plan type Referral usually needed?
Original Medicare (Part B) No
Medicare Advantage HMO Usually yes
Medicare Advantage PPO Usually no
Medicaid managed care (for example Healthfirst, Fidelis Care, MetroPlusHealth, UnitedHealthcare Community Plan) Depends on the plan and the specialty
Fee-for-service Medicaid Generally no
Employer or marketplace HMO Usually yes
Employer or marketplace PPO Usually no

Plans change their rules, sometimes yearly. Use this table as a starting point, then confirm with your plan.

Does Medicare require a referral for pain management?

Original Medicare does not require a referral to see a specialist, as long as the doctor accepts Medicare. Medicare Advantage plans are different: according to Medicare.gov, in most cases you need a referral to see a specialist in an HMO plan. PPO-style Medicare Advantage plans usually let you see specialists without one. See does Medicare cover pain management.

Do Medicaid plans in New York require a referral?

It depends on the plan. Most people with Medicaid in New York City are in a managed care plan, and each plan sets its own referral rules. Some require a PCP referral for certain specialties and not others. For example, UnitedHealthcare Community Plan of New York requires PCP referrals for several specialties, including physical medicine and rehabilitation. Check your plan’s member handbook or call member services. Plan guides: Healthfirst, Fidelis Care, MetroPlusHealth, and Medicaid coverage for injections.

What is the difference between a referral and a prior authorization?

A referral is permission from your PCP to see a specialist. A prior authorization is approval from your insurance company for a specific test or treatment, such as an MRI or an injection. You may need neither, one or both. Even when a visit needs no referral, some treatments may still need prior authorization.

How do you check if you need a referral? (a 3-minute call)

Call the member services number on the back of your insurance card and ask:

  1. “Do I need a referral from my primary care doctor to see a pain management specialist?”
  2. “Is [doctor or practice name] in network for my plan?”
  3. “Do injections or physical therapy need prior authorization?”
  4. “Who is my assigned primary care doctor?” (HMO and Medicaid plans often assign one.)

Write down the date, the name of the person you spoke to and a reference number.

What if your plan needs a referral?

Ask your primary care doctor for a referral to pain management. Bring your insurance card, any imaging reports and a list of your medicines. A referral is often valid for a set number of visits or months, so check how long yours lasts.

Frequently asked questions

Can I go to pain management without a referral?

Yes, if your plan allows it. Original Medicare and most PPO plans do not require one. HMO and many Medicaid managed care plans may.

How long does a referral to pain management take?

It varies by doctor’s office. Many are sent within a few days, but ask your PCP’s office when to expect it.

Can a pain management doctor see me before my referral arrives?

If your plan requires a referral, the visit may not be covered without it. Confirm it has been received before your appointment.

Does my referral expire?

Many referrals are valid for a set number of visits or a set time period. Check the dates on yours.

Do I need a new referral every year?

Often yes for HMO and managed care plans. Ask your plan or your PCP.

Does urgent care or the ER need a referral?

No. Emergency care never requires a referral. See [when to see a doctor, urgent care or the ER for leg pain](https://paintherapycare.com/blog/leg-pain-when-to-see-a-doctor/).
